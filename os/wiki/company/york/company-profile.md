# York Space Systems: comprehensive company profile

> **As of:** 2026-07-10. Confidence markers: (V) verified against a cited public source, (A) owner-attested, (I) inferred/analytical. Full sourcing in the [York research log](../../../raw/competitors/2026-07-10-york-competitive-landscape-research.md). Parent company of [All.Space](../all-space/company-profile.md).

## Snapshot

York Space Systems (NYSE: YSS) is a Denver-area (Greenwood Village, Colorado) space and defense prime founded in 2012 to deliver mission-critical spacecraft at scale, faster, and at lower cost. It is one of the only primes with proprietary hardware and software spanning the entire space ecosystem and mission lifecycle: standardized satellite platforms, mission software, ground operations, propulsion, solar power, and (via All.Space) anti-jam user terminals. Its franchise is the Space Development Agency's Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture (PWSA) Transport Layer, where it holds contracts for 126+ satellites. York went public on 2026-01-29. (V)

## History

- **2012:** York Space Systems, LLC founded to create an innovative space technology mission prime for national-security spacecraft at scale. (V)
- **2020-08:** SDA Tranche 0 Transport Layer award (~$94M, 10 satellites); York delivered and launched in ~2.5 years. (V)
- **2022-02:** Selected for SDA Tranche 1 Transport Layer: 42 satellites (alongside Lockheed Martin and Northrop Grumman at 42 each). (V)
- **2022:** $200M OTA for 12 T1DES experimental satellites (tactical SATCOM, advanced waveforms, IBS). (V)
- **2023-06:** Acquired Emergent Space Technologies (mission software and GN&C, 20-year heritage). (V)
- **2023-10-10:** SDA Tranche 2 Transport Layer Alpha award: ~$617M for 62 satellites in eight planes plus ground and five years of operations (Northrop Grumman took the other 38 Alpha satellites at $732M). (V)
- **2024-08:** SDA T2TL-Gamma award: 10 satellites, launch 2027, supporting JADC2. (V)
- **2025:** Delivered 21 Tranche 1 Transport Layer satellites to orbit, the first prime to execute an on-orbit Tranche 1 delivery, a month ahead of its nearest competitor; launched the accelerated Dragoon T1DES mission (contract to orbit in ~7 months); executed NASA's BARD commercial-communications validation mission; demonstrated Link 16 from space (claims to be the only provider to have done so) and cross-vendor optical links. (V)
- **2025-07-18:** Announced acquisition of ATLAS Space Operations (ground software as a service); completed 2025. (V)
- **2026-01-28/29:** Corporate conversion (from AE Industrial's Yellowstone Midco Holdings II, LLC) and **IPO on NYSE as YSS**: 18.5M shares at $34.00, $582.6M net proceeds; post-IPO total liquidity $895.4M. (V)
- **2026-03-12:** Acquired Orbion Space Technology (electric propulsion). (V)
- **2026-05-06:** Announced multiple national-security IDIQ awards across two mission areas, including systems relevant to Golden Dome (programs undisclosed). (V)
- **2026-06-04:** Closed acquisition of Solestial (US-sourced space solar), ~$67M. (V)
- **2026-07-08:** Closed acquisition of [All.Space](../all-space/company-profile.md) (~$300M), adding jam-resistant multi-orbit ground terminals. (V)

## Products and features

### Satellite platforms (common modular architecture)

The three buses share substantially the same core hardware and flight-proven software stack (M-CLASS shares ~75% of technology with the smaller buses), so York scales into new mission classes without redesign and consolidates one supply chain. (V)

| Platform | Payload | Power | Notes |
|----------|---------|-------|-------|
| **S-CLASS** | 85+ kg | 500 W peak (extensible to 1 kW) | 3-axis stabilized; ESPA-compatible; 24" x 24" stowed; the PWSA workhorse |
| **LX-CLASS** | 300+ kg | 1.5 kW peak | ESPA/ESPA-Grande; 34" x 34" stowed |
| **M-CLASS** (2025) | 1,000+ kg | 8+ kW peak | Company claims competitor-class capability "at half the cost"; won a $187M commercial 20+ satellite constellation contract in Feb 2026 |

All platforms support industry-standard payload data interfaces and are designed for mass manufacture for constellations. (V)

### The integrated stack (what the acquisitions built)

- **Mission software and GN&C:** Emergent Space Technologies (2023). (V)
- **Ground segment:** ATLAS Space Operations' Freedom GSaaS platform and global ground station network, eliminating space-to-ground bottlenecks; a key piece of York's Golden Dome architecture. (V)
- **Propulsion:** Orbion Hall-effect electric propulsion in-house (2026). (V)
- **Power:** Solestial silicon solar cells that self-anneal radiation damage on orbit; ~95% US supply chain vs Chinese-dominated gallium/germanium III-V cells with 2+ year lead times. (V)
- **User terminals:** All.Space Hydra multi-orbit anti-jam ESA terminals (2026). (V)
- **Operations:** turnkey constellation management and Mission Operations C2 through the mission lifecycle. (V)

## Competitive advantages

1. **Speed and cost at scale:** Tranche 0 in ~2.5 years; Dragoon from contract to orbit in ~7 months (75% below typical 30-month timelines); claimed ~half the cost of competitors; first prime to deliver Tranche 1 satellites on orbit. (V, company claims for cost)
2. **Standardized common-architecture platforms** spanning 85 kg to 1,000+ kg payloads with one supply chain. (V)
3. **Full-stack vertical integration** purpose-built through M&A: bus + software + ground + propulsion + solar + terminals + operations; one of the only primes with proprietary hardware and software across the whole mission lifecycle. (V/I)
4. **Demonstrated firsts:** Link 16 from space (claimed only provider); cross-vendor optical inter-satellite links; validated NASA's commercial-communications shift (BARD). (V)
5. **Balance sheet:** ~$895M post-IPO liquidity plus a public currency (YSS shares already used in the Solestial and All.Space deals). (V)

## Vulnerabilities and risks

1. **SDA concentration (the big one):** 126+ satellites and the bulk of revenue ride one customer and one acquisition pathway; the 10-Q names SDA as the largest customer. (V)
2. **The Transport Layer is being restructured out from under the incumbents:** SDA loses semi-autonomous status; programs move under Portfolio Acquisition Executives; the Transport Layer becomes "an enclave within the Space Data Network," with $800M budgeted to on-ramp additional vendors. York's tranche-incumbency advantage may not carry over. (V)
3. **SpaceX captured the successor backbone:** the $2.29B Space Data Network award went to Starshield, and AMTI ($4.16B) followed; York's IDIQs are positioning, not yet equivalent program wins. (V/I)
4. **Absent from Tracking Layer primes:** the $3.5B T3TRK round (Dec 2025) split across Lockheed Martin, Rocket Lab, Northrop Grumman, and L3Harris; York has no tracking-layer prime position while missile-defense budgets (Golden Dome) concentrate there. (V/I)
5. **Execution and margin:** gross margin 19.5% with EBITDA still negative in 2025; scaling M-CLASS, integrating five acquisitions, and hitting Tranche 2 delivery schedules simultaneously is a heavy load. Tranche 1 software issues were referenced in trade coverage. (V/I)
