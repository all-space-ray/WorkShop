# Source: Raymond Tayse email voice samples (2.5 years)

## TL;DR

An external AI harness (Claude.ai with mailbox access) read roughly 2.5 years of Raymond Tayse's sent company email and extracted 52 verbatim, redacted samples plus an analysis of his writing patterns. The samples span professional, casual, and terse registers across customers, vendors, peers, leadership, and investors, and the corpus is almost entirely defense/B2B work correspondence. This is the first of two planned extractions; a second run via Fable 5 Max is pending for comparison.

## Key points

- 52 samples, dated approximately 2024-03 to 2026-07, each tagged with register, audience, intent, length, and an authored-by-me flag (51 yes, 1 uncertain).
- Only prose Raymond composed himself was included; quoted/forwarded text, signatures, disclaimers, and third-party documents were excluded or flagged, matching the authorship discipline requested in the extraction prompt.
- Specifics are redacted with placeholders ([NAME], [CLIENT], [VENDOR], [PROGRAM], [PRODUCT], [AMOUNT], [PART#], etc.); wording, punctuation, capitalization, typos, and line breaks are otherwise verbatim.
- The extractor supplied its own "Observed voice patterns" analysis, which the distilled [voice profile](../voice-profile.md) reworks into cited observations rather than adopting wholesale.

## Coverage gaps (per the extractor, confirmed on read)

- Almost no external formal prose to non-government commercial partners.
- No long persuasive/marketing copy authored by Raymond himself (he forwards decks and whitepapers others wrote).
- No public-facing announcements, no substantive apology for a real error, and little personal/HR self-advocacy beyond short logistics.

## Relevance to existing knowledge

- First user filed under [my-voice](../../README.md); creates the `raymond/` voice folder.
- Feeds the [Raymond voice profile](../voice-profile.md), which deliverable skills (docx, executive-briefing, edit-article) consult.
- The universal [writing-style rules](../../../rules/writing-style.md) still apply on top of the profile. Notably the no-em-dash rule does not conflict with Raymond's own habits: the samples show he already avoids em dashes, preferring periods, semicolons, and trailing ellipses.
