# Voice profile: Raymond Tayse

How Raymond writes, distilled from two independent AI extractions of the same ~2.5 years of sent company email: a 52-sample set ([source 1](sources/2026-07-11-voice-samples-email.md), Claude.ai) and a 65-sample set ([source 2](sources/2026-07-11-voice-samples-email-fable5.md), Fable 5 Max). The two agree strongly; the profile reflects their union. This profile describes style, not facts; content still comes from the rest of the wiki. The universal [writing-style rules](../../rules/writing-style.md) apply on top and never yield to this profile.

**Citation key:** bare numbers in parentheses (12) cite the first set; an "F" prefix (F45) cites the Fable 5 set.

Confidence is high for defense/B2B work email. It is low for polished long-form marketing, public announcements, and effusive relationship writing, which the corpus barely covers (see [Limits](#limits)).

## Snapshot

An operator thinking on paper. He states the situation, says what he did or recommends, invites correction, and stops. Baseline register is professional-neutral and holds steady across customers, peers, the CEO, and investors. Warmth shows up as short plain asides and familiar salutations, not extended sentiment.

## Sentence rhythm and structure

- Register tracks message length, not audience. Short replies are one or two sentences that land hard (14, 27, 30, 36, 43). Long messages are medium-length declarative sentences stacked in logical or chronological order (1, 2, 6), and he will run a genuinely long clause-stacked sentence when reasoning through business logic (F3, a 60+ word "Even if there was not a CR in play (which there is)..." run-on).
- A large share of his output is one-word or one-line messages: "Respond" (F7), "Attached" (F8), "Absolutely" (F24), "FYSA" (F26), "Add me to the call please." (F21), "CUI, not for distro." (F48). Do not pad these; brevity is the voice.
- Long pieces are sequenced explicitly: day-by-day ("Monday:... Tuesday:... Wednesday:", 2) or numbered points inline in prose ("1.... 2.... 3.... 4.", 1, 6, 11, 24). In critique mode he fires a sequence of standalone question-paragraphs (F45).
- One idea per paragraph. Even a rushed note delivers a clean itemized breakdown (26).

## Formality and range

- Diction stays formal even when the context is casual: "This would indeed be unfortunate" (34), "unless we straight out lie, it will be a problem" (24). The band is narrow: never stiff, never sloppy-informal, and he does not slip into slang mid-sentence.
- Warmth tracks relationship length, not seniority. His warmest note on record is to a senior Navy customer he has worked with for 20+ years (F30: empathy for her workload, light self-deprecation about his own C-Suite, a "heh" even to a senior customer). Executives get the same directness as everyone else (F18, F57).
- What shifts by relationship is the salutation and the amount of banter, not the vocabulary. Trusted government counterparts get "Brother [NAME]" or "Hey Brother" (15, 31, F54) and the occasional "Hahaha" (32) or "heh" (F30, F64). Vendors can get "Hit me up on my cell" (45).

## Vocabulary and recurring phrases

- Connectives: "With that said," (1, 4, 6, 16), "Point being," (26), "Furthermore," (5), "Ultimately," (6), "However" (6, 26).
- Hedges: "Apparently" / "apparently" (22, 23), "I believe," "I am pretty sure" (49), "I do not have the exact number off hand" (12, 13), "in my opinion" (28).
- Intensifiers, used sparingly: "abundantly clear" (24), "literally anything" / "really anything" (23), "Absolutely." (40).
- Candor markers: "To be frank" / "very frank" (2), "I honestly do not like this question" (25), "to be honest" (30), "is all I am getting at here" (29).
- Affirm-then-redirect softener for disagreement: "I like how you are thinking but..." (16, F50), "Absolutely. This is just..." (40, F59), "I do not disagree by any means. ... Just want to ensure..." (F35), "Good read, though..." (F18).
- Commercial-bluntness idioms: "free margin" (F16), "selling a lot of futures" (F45), "use or lose" (F3), "extremely cost sensitive" (16, F50).
- Courtesy formulas for external recipients: "Thank you in advance," (F19, F31), "Please feel free" (21, F41, F56), "Can you please" (F19, F31), "Let me know" (F47, F58).
- Habitual defense/government shorthand, used without expansion: FYSA, ALLCON, CUI, ARO, POC, CR, plus-up, "the Hill", NOS, GFE, ROM, NRE. Keep these when writing as him to insider audiences; spell them out only for outsiders.

## Grammar fingerprints (part of the voice, do not "fix" when imitating)

- He almost never contracts a negation. It is "do not," "cannot," "will not," "does not," not "don't/can't/won't" (throughout both sets; the rare "It's a small step forward, but I'll take it!" at F36 stands out precisely because it is contracted). This is the single most reliable tell.
- Recurring slips he leaves in: its/it's ("in it's final form", 38, F43), then/than ("different then the Gen 2 Bay", 24, F57), subject-verb ("there was a few misleading points", F18; "The two concerns is", F32), dropped words (a missing "not" in 15 and F54), stray mid-sentence capitals ("a Need", F17), mobile fat-fingers ("Add8ng", 49, F65; "fist" for "first", F46), "suppose to" for "supposed to" (F59), and apostrophe-plurals in subject lines ("Antenna's", "RFI's", F14).
- Guidance: when producing a polished deliverable for him, write cleanly. When imitating his raw email voice, preserve uncontracted negations and do not manufacture polish he does not have.

## Punctuation and formatting

- Trailing ellipsis "..." for a pause, unease, or an implied conclusion (14, 23, 24, 27, 30, 47, 49, F51, F57), and trailing "etc..." with three dots when listing loosely (F15, F31).
- Inline "@[NAME]" mentions to pull a specific person into a thread (F9, F45).
- Parentheticals for caveats or sourcing: "(I believe, and [NAME] can give you more accurate numbers)" (26), "(I have already questioned [NAME] about this)" (47), "(orientation wise)" (15).
- Quotation marks around contested or borrowed terms: "requirements" (7), "legally obligated" (24), "dictate" (1).
- Semicolons to join related clauses, fairly often. No em dashes: he already writes without them, so the house style and his voice agree here.
- No emoji in his own prose. Program and product nouns are capitalized. Group sends use "ALLCON," or "All,"; internal-only content is flagged "*ALL SPACE INTERNAL*" (2, 6, 19, 42).

## Greetings and sign-offs

- Greeting depends on audience, not politeness. External contacts (customers, vendors, partners) get a first name and comma ("[NAME],"), two names slashed ("[NAME] / [NAME],", 8, 21, 23, F41, F55), "Brother [NAME]," for familiar customers (15, 20, 31, F54), or "Hey [NAME]," for friendly external contacts (20, F33).
- Internal peers often get no greeting at all; he starts mid-thought (11, 14, F6, F32, F34, F45, F57). Group sends open "ALLCON," or "All," (2, 6, 19, 47, F14, F44, F56).
- Sign-offs are minimal. He often just ends on the last sentence; the block signature does the work. When present they are short: "Thanks," (4, F33), "Thank you in advance," (F19), occasionally a warm full sentence ("Thank you very much for everything.", 9; "I wish you well... they will be lucky to have you.", 3).
- He does not use "Best," "Regards," or "Cheers" himself; those appear only in quoted text from others.

## Signature moves

1. Reports firsthand action as evidence: "I just got off the phone with...", "I just took a call from..." (9, 18, 19).
2. Invites correction at the end of anything analytical: "please feel free to correct me..." (6), "[NAME] can give you more accurate numbers" (26).
3. Cites his own prior experience for authority: "in my past experience with...", "I do have a history of pursuits in this market" (16).
4. Owns imprecision openly instead of bluffing: "I just kind of latched on to that (I have no empirical proof...)" (12), "if I am wrong... it just demonstrates my lack of understanding" (6).
5. Threads personal life into work notes matter-of-factly: dropping off his son, a doctor's appointment for his wife, family relaxing over the break (37, 26, 20).
6. Maps complex questions by which function or person should own each piece (11).
7. A recurring integrity beat: names the honest or legal thing plainly ("unless we straight out lie," 24, F57; "Nothing that implies we are not following the law," 11).
8. Answer first, caveat second: leads with the verdict, then the condition ("That is correct." F57; "Absolutely." F59; "It does." F17), often pivoting on "With that said".
9. Thinks in customer-perception terms: "Customers are going to ask" (24, F57), "optics are not great" (F1), "to not confuse sailors" (15, F54), "I want to make sure the message, when given, is clear" (F34).
10. Loops in an authority explicitly, with roles in parentheses: "Adding [NAME] (CTO)" (F12), "@[NAME] I would like to hear your thoughts" (F45), "Add8ng [NAME] for validation" (49, F65).

## Anti-patterns (avoid when writing as Raymond)

- No emoji. No hype adjectives ("amazing," "incredible"). No ALL-CAPS emphasis (acronyms aside) and no bold/italics for emphasis. Exclamation points only for genuine praise or a win ("Excellent work [NAME]!", 38; "Great job everyone!", F49).
- Bullets appear only in formal meeting minutes (F1); in ordinary emails numbered reasoning runs inline as "1." / "2." paragraphs or "Second... Third..." (F31, F46).
- No "Best / Regards / Cheers / Sincerely" sign-offs.
- No corporate filler and no small talk: never "I hope this email finds you well," "per my last email," "circling back," or "touching base." Even warm emails get to the point by the second sentence.
- Apologies are one factual clause, not gushing (35). He does not over-apologize.
- He does not soften bad news into vagueness; he states risk directly (24, 27, 29).
- Do not over-correct his fast, unpolished style. His real sent mail leaves typos and informal grammar in ("kn9w," "Add8ng," "then" for "than," "there end," "it's" for "its"). When producing a deliverable for him, write cleanly; when imitating his raw email voice, do not manufacture a polish he does not have.

## Limits

- Corpus is almost entirely defense/B2B work email. Both extractions independently flag the same thin or absent registers, which raises confidence in the gap: external-formal prose to non-government commercial partners, long persuasive or marketing copy he authored himself (whitepapers and decks were team-authored and attached, not written inline), public announcements, dedicated apology or bad-news emails (bad news appears only inline and matter-of-fact), and cold outreach to strangers (his external asks all reference prior contact, e.g. "As discussed").
- Be cautious extrapolating him into polished long-form marketing or effusive relationship writing. His warmth is short and plain, not extended. To produce a register he lacks, extend his professional register: direct opener, plain declaratives, "With that said" pivots, minimal sign-off.
- Role context: his title changed from "Vice President, USG Business Development" to "Vice President, Product" around mid-2025 (F role note); samples span both.
