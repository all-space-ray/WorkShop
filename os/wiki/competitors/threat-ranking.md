# All.Space competitive threat ranking

> **As of 2026-07-10.** Ranking weights Ka-band and electronically steered antenna (ESA) manufacturers as the biggest threats, per direction. Scoring is analytical (I) on verified inputs (V) captured in the [research log](../../raw/competitors/2026-07-10-competitive-landscape-research.md).

## Scoring model

Each competitor is scored 1-5 (5 = highest threat) on five weighted dimensions:

| Dimension | Weight | What it measures |
|-----------|--------|------------------|
| Product overlap (ESA / multi-orbit / Ka) | 30% | How directly the product competes with Hydra |
| DoD/program momentum | 25% | Wins in the accounts All.Space targets |
| Technology maturity / fielded status | 20% | Shipping vs prototype vs concept |
| Scale and capital | 15% | Manufacturing + balance sheet to sustain competition |
| Account collision with All.Space | 10% | Head-to-head in the same customers/programs |

## The three findings that matter most

1. **Kymeta is the clear and present danger.** It is not just building a comparable product; it is winning the exact US Navy and Army accounts All.Space targets, through the same prime (Bascom Hunter), with a new DoD-veteran CEO. Any All.Space/York account plan must treat Kymeta as the primary opponent. (V)
2. **The threat is bimodal.** Ranks 1-6 are direct ESA/terminal competitors racing on multi-orbit Ka; ranks 8-10 are structural threats (network owners and modem owners who can bundle or restrict access). All.Space must win the terminal race AND defend the merchant model against vertical integration. (I)
3. **Anti-jam resilience is All.Space's defensible moat.** Every credible competitor can do multi-orbit switching; the lens-array simultaneous full-duplex + spatial-filtering anti-jam story is what York bought and what differentiates in contested environments. Messaging and proof points should concentrate there. (I)
