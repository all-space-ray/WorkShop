# York Space Systems competitive threat ranking

> **As of 2026-07-10.** Ranks the ten most significant competitors to [York Space Systems](../company/york/company-profile.md) in its core market: proliferated national-security satellite constellations (SDA PWSA and its Space Data Network successor), missile warning/tracking/defense (Golden Dome), and integrated mission operations. Scoring is analytical (I) on verified inputs (V) captured in the [York research log](../../raw/competitors/2026-07-10-york-competitive-landscape-research.md). Companion ranking for the All.Space terminal market: [threat-ranking.md](threat-ranking.md).

## Scoring model

Each competitor is scored 1-5 (5 = highest threat) on five weighted dimensions, adapted from the All.Space model to York's market:

| Dimension | Weight | What it measures |
|-----------|--------|------------------|
| Program overlap (proliferated constellations / SDA / SDN) | 30% | How directly the offering competes for York's programs |
| DoD/program momentum | 25% | Recent wins in the budgets York targets (SDA, SDN, Golden Dome, MDA) |
| Production maturity / fielded status | 20% | Satellites delivered and operated vs prototypes |
| Scale and capital | 15% | Manufacturing capacity + balance sheet to sustain competition |
| Account collision with York | 10% | Head-to-head in the same competitions and customers |

## The four findings that matter most

1. **The moat is being drained, not stormed.** York's franchise was built on SDA's tranche-based, fixed-price incumbency model. That model is ending: SDA is folding under Portfolio Acquisition Executives and the Transport Layer becomes "an enclave within the Space Data Network," whose $2.29B backbone went to SpaceX, with $800M earmarked to on-ramp new vendors. York's top risk is architectural, not any single rival's product. (V/I)
2. **York is absent from the Tracking Layer, where the money is going.** The December 2025 T3TRK round put $3.5B across Lockheed Martin, Rocket Lab, Northrop Grumman, and L3Harris; Golden Dome concentrates further spending on missile warning/tracking/defense and interceptors. York's IDIQs and its All.Space/ATLAS/Orbion stack are its Golden Dome positioning, but it has no tracking prime seat today. (V/I)
3. **The primes have copied the York playbook.** Lockheed bought Terran Orbital for captive high-rate production; Northrop partnered with Apex for agility; Rocket Lab built a vertically integrated new-prime stack with launch York lacks. York's original differentiators (speed, cost, standardized buses) are now table stakes; its remaining edge is delivered scale (126+ satellites), operational heritage, and the integrated ecosystem it acquired in 2025-2026. (I)
4. **Defend with interoperability and full-stack economics.** The Pentagon's stated intent to on-ramp multiple vendors, York's demonstrated cross-vendor optical links and Link 16 from space, and its half-cost claims are the counter-narrative to a SpaceX-dominated SDN; the ATLAS+Orbion+Solestial+All.Space stack turns supply-chain control into a security argument (US-sourced solar vs Chinese gallium, domestic propulsion, anti-jam terminals). Concentrate proof points there. (I)
