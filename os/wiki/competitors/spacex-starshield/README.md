# SpaceX Starshield / Amazon Leo (vertically integrated constellations)

> **All.Space threat rank: 10 of 10 (STRATEGIC / VERTICAL).** **York threat rank: 1 of 10 (CRITICAL)**, see the [York section](#as-a-york-space-systems-competitor-critical-rank-1-of-10) below. As of 2026-07-10. Grouped because both pose the same structural threat to All.Space: constellation owners bundling their own terminals.

## Snapshot

These are not ESA-terminal peers; they are constellation operators that bundle their own phased-array user terminals, threatening the entire third-party ("merchant") terminal model that All.Space depends on. SpaceX Starshield is the militarized, encrypted variant of Starlink. Amazon Leo (rebranded from Project Kuiper in November 2025) had 390+ satellites by July 2026 with commercial service targeted ~Q3 2026. (V)

## Why this is a strategic threat

- **Vertical integration + bundling:** each operator supplies its own terminal with its own network, commoditizing connectivity and squeezing independent terminal makers on price and access. (I)
- **Precedent already felt:** Starlink withdrew third-party antenna support in mid-2025, directly disrupting programs that planned to ride Starlink through independent terminals (the origin of All.Space's pivot to Amazon Leo Ka + WGS). (A/V)
- **Band note:** Starlink/Starshield are primarily Ku; Amazon Leo is Ka-capable. Band-adjacent rather than head-to-head ESA, but existential to the merchant model. (V/I)

## Countervailing factors (why not rank higher on direct product threat)

- Closed ecosystems: their terminals serve their own constellations, not the multi-orbit, multi-network, cross-vendor resilience that tactical/defense customers require. (I)
- Defense customers explicitly want vendor/constellation diversity and anti-jam resilience in contested environments, which is precisely All.Space's and York's thesis (single-network reliance is a vulnerability). (V, per York rationale)

## All.Space counter-position

All.Space's whole value proposition is the opposite of single-network lock-in: one device connecting to all of these networks simultaneously with anti-jam resilience. The strategic risk is that operators restrict third-party access; the opportunity is that their restrictions make multi-orbit, multi-vendor terminals more valuable to defense. (I)

## As a York Space Systems competitor (CRITICAL, rank 1 of 10)

SpaceX is the single largest threat to York's franchise, sourced in the [York research log](../../../raw/competitors/2026-07-10-york-competitive-landscape-research.md):

- **Captured the successor to York's core program:** the **$2.29B Space Force Space Data Network backbone** (formerly MILNET) will be built on Starshield satellites and operated by the Space Force, with a fully operational prototype required by end-2028. The SDA Transport Layer York built 126+ satellites for becomes "an enclave" within this SpaceX-built network. (V)
- **$4.16B AMTI award (2026):** proliferated LEO constellation to track airborne targets, initial capability by 2028; FY2027 seeks $7.1B for AMTI. (V)
- Starshield scale: 183+ satellites launched by 2025; $1.8B classified NRO constellation (with Northrop Grumman) operational from May 2024; 8 Tranche 0 Tracking satellites heritage; among the 12 Golden Dome SBI prototype awardees; SpaceX filed for a potentially record IPO (2026). (V)
- **Why critical for York:** vertical integration (launch + bus + network + operations) at unmatched cost, and procurement momentum that bypasses the tranche-based model York's incumbency was built on. (I)
- **York counter-position:** the Pentagon explicitly budgets $800M to "on-ramp additional vendors" into the SDN; multi-vendor policy, interoperability standards, and concerns about single-provider dependence are York's openings. York's counter is proven multi-vendor interoperability (cross-vendor optical links, Link 16) plus cost discipline. (V/I)
